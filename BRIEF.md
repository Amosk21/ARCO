# ARCO: Machine-Verifiable Regulatory Risk Classification

**Current implementation: EU AI Act Annex III. For compliance officers and legal counsel deploying AI in Annex III-adjacent use cases.**

---

## The Problem

Annex III classification is a legal determination, not an engineering judgment. Providers who assert a system is — or is not — high-risk must document that assessment before market entry and defend it to national competent authorities on demand.

Current practice: legal opinion, internal review, spreadsheet checklists.
None of these are formally auditable, reproducible, or machine-verifiable.

When a regulator asks *why* your system was classified as it was, the answer cannot be "our legal team reviewed it."

---

## What ARCO Produces

ARCO applies OWL-RL reasoning over a BFO/CCO-aligned ontology to produce a **deterministic, entailment-based classification**. The output is not generated by an LLM or heuristic. Every conclusion follows from formal axioms over a machine-readable system description.

Each determination includes:

- A signed certificate naming the triggering capability, evidence path, and classification
- Two verification layers: OWL-RL classification (the formal entailment that produces the determination) and SPARQL ASK audit queries that run on the reasoned graph to confirm documentary content is explicitly declared and aligned with the regulatory condition — each boolean, each traceable
- A SHACL validation report confirming structural completeness of the system description
- A traceable chain: system capabilities → triggering classification → applicable obligations → provider role

## Example: Remote Biometric Identity Verification

**System:** AI-based facial recognition used for remote customer onboarding in financial services.

| Gate | Formal Condition | Status |
|------|-----------------|--------|
| 1 — Reality | Hardware component bears `BiometricIdentificationCapability` (disposition-level, reality-side) | ✓ PASS |
| 2 — Intent | IntendedUseSpecification `cco:prescribes` an instance typed as `RemoteBiometricIdentificationProcess` — type-checked via `owl:someValuesFrom`, not IRI-matched | ✓ PASS |
| 3 — Scope | UseScenarioSpecification references `NaturalPersonRole` as the affected role category — the universal, not a specific role-bearer | ✓ PASS |

**ARCO determination (OWL-RL entailed, not asserted):**

```
CLASSIFICATION:     HighRiskSystem (INFERRED)
ANNEX III 1(a):     VERIFIED (ENTAILED)
TRIGGERING CAP:     BiometricIdentificationCapability
SHACL:              PASS
TRACEABILITY:       PASS
OBLIGATION:         ComplianceObligations → ProviderRole (LINKED)
ENTAILED TRIPLES:   +742
```

Each gate checks specific content, not the existence of documentation alone. Gate 2 requires the intended use document to prescribe a process that is actually typed as the regulated process class — a system documented as intended for on-site fingerprint enrollment would fail Gate 2 and not be classified under 1(a), even if it has biometric capability. Gate 3 requires the scenario to explicitly reference the affected role category. If any gate fails — capability not present, wrong process type prescribed, role category absent — the entailment does not fire. The system is not classified as high-risk, and the reasoning why is documented in the same format.

## Why This Matters Under the Act

| Requirement | What ARCO Produces |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Art. 6 — Classification rationale | Formal entailment chain, not legal opinion |
| Art. 9 — Risk management system | Traceable classification evidence that can support, but does not replace, a broader Article 9 risk-management process |
| Art. 11 — Technical documentation | Reproducible classification evidence with provenance that can contribute to, but does not itself constitute, full Article 11 documentation |
| Art. 49 — Registration of high-risk and certain non-high-risk systems | Same pipeline can support a documented non-high-risk conclusion, but Article 49 is a registration obligation tied to EU database entries |

The output is not a checklist. It is a formal artifact that can be handed to a notified body, a national competent authority, or an internal audit team — and reproduced exactly on any subsequent run against the same system description.

## Current Scope

- **Covered:** EU AI Act Annex III, category 1(a) — remote biometric identification of natural persons
- **Pipeline:** OWL-RL reasoning (owlrl) + SHACL (pySHACL) + SPARQL ASK (RDFLib) — no LLMs
- **Standards alignment:** BFO 2020, CCO, IAO, RO — imported upper ontology, no proprietary vocabulary
- **Status:** Working pipeline, open for evaluation on real system descriptions

## Who This Is For

- Compliance officers and DPOs at organizations deploying biometric AI — especially where Annex III 1(a) applicability is disputed
- Legal counsel advising on pre-market classification decisions
- Notified bodies and auditors building evaluation frameworks
- Internal AI governance teams needing a defensible, reproducible classification record
